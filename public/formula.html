Magic-square basics

The formula

The magic constant

Every order-n magic square uses the whole numbers 1 through n² exactly once. Their total is fixed, so dividing it evenly across the n rows gives the number every row, column, and diagonal must sum to:

M(n) = n(n² + 1) / 2

For example, a 3×3 square uses 1–9 (sum 45), split across 3 rows → M(3) = 15. A 9×9 square uses 1–81 → M(9) = 369. This formula holds for any n — it tells you the target sum even for sizes where no valid arrangement exists (like n=2).

Why 2×2 is impossible

With 4 distinct numbers, a 2×2 square needs both rows, both columns, and both diagonals to share one sum. Diagonal cells overlap with every row and column, which over-constrains the 4 cells until no arrangement of distinct numbers can satisfy all six conditions simultaneously. It's the one order that's provably not magic — every other order from 3 up can be constructed.

Build a magic square from YOUR date of birth

Srinivasa Ramanujan was famous for constructing a 4×4 magic square live from a birthdate, with the date sitting unmodified in the top row. Split the date into DD (day), MM (month), CC (century), and YY (two-digit year), then fill the grid with this classical pattern:

DD      MM      CC      YY
YY + 1  CC - 1  MM - 3  DD + 3
MM - 2  DD + 2  YY + 2  CC - 2
CC + 1  YY - 1  DD + 1  MM - 1

Every row, every column, and both diagonals always sum to DD + MM + CC + YY. Some of those offsets (−1, −2, −3) are negative, so a date with a small month (Jan/Feb/Mar), a small century, or a small year-tail (like "01") can push a cell to zero or below — not a natural number. When that happens, the whole square is shifted up by one uniform amount first: this keeps the magic property exact (every sum just goes up by the same 4×shift), and it's the only case where the top row won't literally match your typed-in date anymore.

Because the four numbers come from your actual birthdate, this general version can't guarantee all 16 cells come out distinct for every possible date (some dates cause two cells to coincide) — but the row/column/diagonal magic property always holds.

Nothing here is saved or sent anywhere except to compute the square — your date of birth isn't stored.
